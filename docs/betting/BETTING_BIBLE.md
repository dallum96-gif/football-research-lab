# FRL Betting Bible

## Purpose

FRL Betting exists to make disciplined, evidence-led football betting decisions.

The objective is not to maximise the number of bets, maximise the strike rate, or manufacture impressive-looking accumulators.

The objective is:

**Long-run profitability through selective bets that FRL has strong, defensible reasons to believe in.**

FRL should prefer bets that can win frequently and whose return is sensible for the risk taken. A high hit rate is useful only when the price still permits long-run profit.

---

## 1. Start With Football, Not Bets

FRL analyses the match before searching for betting markets.

First ask:

- What kind of match do we expect?
- Which team is likely to control territory and possession?
- Where should chances and other repeatable actions come from?
- Which players' roles are most relevant?
- What does each side consistently produce and concede?
- What tactical, lineup, availability, schedule, or game-state factors could change the usual pattern?

Only after forming a view of the football should FRL look for markets that express it.

---

## 2. Find Beliefs Before Finding Bets

A bet should represent an underlying football belief.

Example belief:

> Liverpool should sustain heavy attacking pressure and generate repeated penalty-area entries.

Possible expressions could include team goals, team shots, team corners, individual attacker shot markets, or opposition goalkeeper saves.

FRL should prefer the cleanest and most robust expression of the underlying belief rather than the market with the most exciting potential return.

---

## 3. Evidence Must Converge

Strong selections should normally be supported from several genuinely different directions.

Relevant evidence may include:

- player baseline;
- team baseline;
- opposition concessions;
- positional matchup;
- tactical role;
- home/away behaviour;
- expected minutes;
- recent role changes;
- game-state expectations;
- lineup and injury information;
- schedule and rotation context;
- longer-term historical performance;
- model output where a governed model exists.

Multiple statistics describing essentially the same phenomenon do not count as independent evidence.

FRL should distinguish genuine convergence from repeatedly measuring the same signal in different ways.

---

## 4. Challenge Every Bet

FRL must actively search for reasons a candidate selection could fail.

For every serious candidate ask:

**What is the strongest argument against this bet?**

Potential failure modes include:

- unusual starting XI;
- reduced expected minutes;
- tactical change;
- misleading recent sample;
- opponent-specific suppression;
- dependence on one rare event;
- excessive sensitivity to game state;
- a market threshold that is slightly too aggressive;
- unavailable or immature data;
- an assumption that looks strong descriptively but has not proved predictive.

A strong bet is one that survives serious scrutiny, not one for which FRL can collect the most supporting statistics.

---

## 5. Prefer Robust Over Fragile

FRL should favour bets with multiple routes to success.

Where the evidence supports the distinction, prefer repeatable volume and behaviour over rare conversion events.

For example, a player's shot volume may be more predictable than whether one of those shots becomes a goal.

Specificity should only be added when the evidence justifies it.

---

## 6. No Forced Bets

**No bet is a successful analytical outcome.**

FRL does not need to produce:

- one bet per match;
- one bet per day;
- a bet builder for every televised fixture;
- a fixed number of selections.

If nothing clears the required standard, FRL should say so.

---

## 7. Rank, Don't Accumulate

Candidates should compete against one another.

Ask:

**If we could place only one bet from this match, which would it be?**

Adding additional selections requires additional evidence.

The existence of one strong bet does not imply that four strong bets exist.

---

## 8. Bet Builders Require Greater Scrutiny

Every additional leg creates another route to failure.

FRL should not improve an apparently attractive price by casually adding plausible legs.

Before adding a leg ask:

**Does this materially improve the betting proposition, or are we simply becoming greedy?**

Simple singles should be preferred when they better express the analysis.

### Correlation rule

Leg probabilities must not be multiplied and treated as a calibrated whole-bet probability merely because obvious dependency clashes have been removed.

Same-game legs can retain positive or negative correlation through shared match state, team dominance, player roles, substitutions, score effects, and tactical changes.

Any multiplied builder probability shown by FRL is therefore **indicative unless a governed joint-probability method explicitly models the dependency structure**.

For price decisions on a builder, use a probability estimate that FRL deliberately endorses for the **whole bet**.

---

## 9. Probability Matters

FRL should think probabilistically even when exact probability estimates are uncertain.

Do not use false precision simply because a percentage is convenient.

Initial confidence classes may include:

### Strong
FRL would be genuinely surprised if the selection failed. The football case is clear, evidence converges, and the principal risks have been considered.

### Good
Substantial evidence supports the selection, but meaningful uncertainty remains.

### Interesting
A defensible hypothesis, but below the normal FRL recommendation standard.

### Avoid
Insufficient evidence, excessive fragility, poor circumstances, an unattractive price, or a material unresolved risk.

Only Strong and exceptional Good selections should normally become recommendations.

---

## 10. Price Still Matters

FRL is not required to hunt microscopic theoretical edges, but profitability requires a relationship between probability and price.

A highly likely event can still be a poor bet at an extremely short price. A lower-probability event can be a better betting proposition when the offered return more than compensates for the risk.

Before betting ask:

**Is the potential return sensible given the probability we believe and the uncertainty around that belief?**

### FRL price discipline

When FRL supplies an endorsed probability for a selection or whole builder:

1. Convert that probability into fair odds.
2. Apply a small safety haircut when appropriate to allow for estimation error.
3. Require a minimum positive edge above the conservative break-even price.
4. Bet only if the bookmaker price clears that threshold.

The default calculator settings are a starting discipline, not immutable truth. They should be reviewed against FRL's recorded calibration and profitability.

---

## 11. Results Do Not Prove Individual Decisions Right or Wrong

A good bet can lose. A bad bet can win.

Selections should therefore be reviewed based on:

- reasoning quality;
- information available before kickoff;
- whether the underlying assumptions were correct;
- whether the main risks were identified;
- whether the confidence level was justified;
- whether the accepted price was sensible.

Outcome remains important, but it should not be allowed to rewrite the original reasoning after the event.

---

## 12. Keep Records

For every official FRL recommendation record at minimum:

- date;
- fixture;
- selection;
- odds taken;
- stake;
- confidence;
- endorsed probability where one is used;
- reasoning summary;
- principal pre-match risk;
- result;
- profit/loss.

Over time analyse:

- strike rate;
- return on stakes / ROI;
- profit and loss;
- calibration of probability estimates;
- performance by market;
- performance by confidence class;
- performance by league;
- performance by odds range;
- singles versus builders;
- common causes of failure.

FRL should learn from its own betting history rather than from memorable wins and losses.

---

## 13. Update the Bible

This document is a living methodology, not permanent doctrine.

Update it when evidence shows that:

- a market performs particularly well;
- a supposedly strong indicator is unreliable;
- FRL systematically over- or under-estimates a probability class;
- a new model materially improves decision-making;
- recurring analytical mistakes are identified;
- a default calculator assumption is poorly calibrated.

Changes should be based on evidence rather than individual wins or losses.

---

# The FRL Betting Test

Before recommending a bet, ask:

1. **What do we believe will happen in the football match?**
2. **Why do we believe it?**
3. **Do genuinely different evidence sources support it?**
4. **What is the strongest case against it?**
5. **What is the least fragile betting expression of the belief?**
6. **Would we choose this if we could place only one bet?**
7. **Is the potential return sensible for the risk and uncertainty?**

If those questions cannot be answered convincingly:

**Do not bet.**

---

# Standard FRL Betting Session Instruction

Use this document as the governing methodology whenever an FRL betting session begins.

Act as both an elite football analyst and a disciplined bettor. Challenge proposed selections rather than agreeing with them automatically.

The objective is long-run profit from selective, high-confidence betting — not an arbitrary strike rate, forced bets, or accumulators built for entertainment.

For each fixture, do the analytical complexity underneath and return a simple conclusion:

- the strongest selection or selections;
- why FRL believes them;
- the principal reason each could fail;
- the price threshold where relevant;
- anything that should be avoided.

If nothing is strong enough, say **no bet**.

If adding a leg or selection materially weakens the proposition, say so.

When pricing a bet builder, use an explicitly endorsed probability for the **whole builder** rather than assuming multiplied leg probabilities are calibrated.

---

# Core Principle

**Do not search for reasons to place a bet. Search for football conclusions strong enough to deserve one.**
