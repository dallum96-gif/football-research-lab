# FRL Competitive Intelligence Standard

**Status:** Standing project standard  
**Date:** 5 September 2026

## Purpose

FRL is not to be judged against the standard of a personal statistics project.

For every significant analytical, modelling, data or product capability, ask:

> **What would professional-grade look like here?**

Then assess the gap honestly and decide which parts are worth closing now.

## Continuous benchmark set

Relevant comparison points include, where public evidence exists:

- Stats Perform / Opta;
- Hudl / StatsBomb / Wyscout;
- SkillCorner;
- SciSports;
- Twenty First Group;
- football analytics consultancies;
- professional club analysis and recruitment departments;
- publicly described betting-syndicate and quantitative-market methods;
- academic football modelling;
- strong independent analysts;
- public football data-science and open-source projects;
- adjacent sports and quantitative disciplines where methods transfer.

Commercial prominence is not treated as proof of methodological superiority.

## Separate the source of advantage

Every comparison should distinguish at least:

1. **Data advantage** — richer, cleaner, more granular, broader or more timely observations.
2. **Modelling advantage** — stronger estimation, prediction, calibration, simulation or uncertainty treatment.
3. **Engineering advantage** — reliability, scale, reproducibility, latency, integration and research velocity.
4. **Football-domain advantage** — better concepts, role definitions, tactical interpretation or question selection.
5. **Interface advantage** — better progression from discovery to evidence to decision.
6. **Brand/distribution advantage** — trust, sales, access and presentation that may be independent of analytical quality.

Do not imitate one category when the actual competitor advantage comes from another.

## Required capability review

Before or during every major FRL milestone, answer:

- What is the football/research decision this capability is meant to improve?
- How do serious professional or academic systems approach the same problem?
- What appears genuinely excellent rather than merely polished?
- Which methods are publicly evidenced and which would be speculation about proprietary systems?
- What can FRL reproduce with its present evidence?
- What requires data FRL does not possess?
- What can FRL do differently or better through transparency, provenance, reproducibility or research flexibility?
- What would constitute a credible professional-grade acceptance test?

## Research rule

Bring back synthesis, not link collections.

For a significant external idea, record:

1. what it is;
2. why it may work;
3. public evidence for its use or value;
4. limitations and required data;
5. whether FRL should adopt, test, defer or reject it;
6. how it fits existing FRL architecture;
7. how success would be measured.

## Modelling rule

A sophisticated method does not earn adoption because a professional organisation uses it.

FRL should preserve strong baselines and require challengers to demonstrate incremental value under the same chronological, coverage and scoring rules.

Professional practice is evidence about what is worth testing, not permission to skip testing.

## Product rule

Do not copy visual decoration.

Study professional workflows for how they let an analyst move through:

**Question → filter/context → comparison → exact evidence → interpretation → saved decision/research object.**

FRL should make deep evidence easier to interrogate without hiding uncertainty or provenance.

## Data-gap rule

If a professional capability depends on observations FRL cannot currently make, state the boundary explicitly.

For example, aggregate event/team statistics should not be stretched into claims about compactness, pressure geometry, off-ball runs or decision options that require tracking/spatial action data.

A data limitation is a roadmap input, not an invitation to manufacture a proxy.

## Operating expectation

Competitive intelligence is continuous rather than a one-off market survey.

When FRL begins a significant new capability, external reconnaissance should happen early enough to influence design. When the capability is complete, compare the result against the benchmark again.

The aim is not to look professional.

The aim is to build methods, evidence and workflows that can withstand professional comparison.
