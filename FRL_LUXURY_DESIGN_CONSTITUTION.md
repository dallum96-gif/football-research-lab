# Football Research Laboratory — Luxury Design Constitution

**Status:** active product-design standard
**First reference implementation:** Fixtures premium pass

## Intent

FRL should feel beautiful, expensive, calm and deliberate.

The visual reference is not a generic SaaS dashboard, AI product, sportsbook or game HUD. The closer mental model is a high-end editorial product: fashion house, architectural publication, private members' club, premium financial journal or beautifully typeset sporting almanac.

Luxury should come from restraint, proportion, typography, whitespace, rhythm and confidence — not decoration.

> **FRL should look expensive even in black and white.**

## Hard rejection rules

Reject a design if it relies on any of the following as its main visual language:

- gradients;
- glassmorphism;
- neon or glowing borders;
- floating dashboard cards everywhere;
- oversized KPI tiles;
- excessive pills, badges or chips;
- icon-heavy navigation;
- gamified HUD styling;
- generic rounded SaaS controls;
- heavy shadows;
- decorative animation without analytical purpose;
- rainbow metric colouring;
- large empty hero sections that exist only to look 'modern';
- component grids that make the page feel assembled rather than composed.

If a page could plausibly be mistaken for a generic AI startup template, redesign it.

## Positive design principles

### 1. Compose pages; do not stack components

Each page should have an intentional visual rhythm. Use hierarchy, alignment, white space and rules to structure the page before reaching for containers.

Do not automatically put information in a card.

### 2. Typography carries status

Use type scale, weight, spacing and alignment to create hierarchy. Important football information should feel editorial rather than labelled by UI chrome.

Large type is reserved for genuinely important information: entity identity, fixture identity, key result or decisive analytical conclusion.

### 3. Whitespace is functional

Whitespace should separate levels of meaning and make dense evidence easier to scan. It is not wasted space.

### 4. Use rules before boxes

Prefer hairline dividers, section rules and alignment over bordered containers.

### 5. Colour is scarce

The warm FRL palette remains the base. Accent colours are signals, not decoration. Most interfaces should work in near-monochrome before accent is added.

### 6. Interaction should be quiet

Hover, selection and focus states should feel precise and controlled. Avoid bounce, glow, scale-up spectacle or gratuitous motion.

### 7. Football first

Team names, kits, scores, dates, match context and analytical conclusions should dominate. Interface labels should recede.

### 8. Data density can still feel premium

Luxury does not mean sparse functionality. Tables, rankings, fixture histories and research evidence may be dense, but they should be typeset like a publication rather than rendered like an admin panel.

### 9. Contextual complexity

Global navigation stays simple. Detailed controls appear only where relevant to the current page or task.

### 10. Provenance remains available without dominating

FRL remains a research product. Evidence quality, missingness, limitations and provenance must stay inspectable, but they should not visually overwhelm the primary football task.

## Product character

FRL should feel:

- assured rather than flashy;
- editorial rather than dashboard-like;
- analytical rather than gamified;
- warm rather than sterile;
- crafted rather than generated;
- premium rather than ornate;
- distinctive rather than fashionable for its own sake.

## Fixtures reference

The Fixtures page should behave like a premium football ledger or almanac:

- strong club identity;
- elegant season control;
- restrained record summary;
- filtering integrated into the page rather than placed in a dashboard box;
- month headings with generous rhythm;
- fixture rows that read like editorial records;
- score and result typography carrying the main emphasis;
- team kits used sparingly as football context;
- provenance and coverage retained at the bottom as research metadata.

This page is the first concrete reference for applying the constitution across Teams, Players, Rankings, Matchday and betting surfaces.
