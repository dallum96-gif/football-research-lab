# Football Research Laboratory — Luxury Design Constitution

**Status:** active product-design standard  
**Primary visual anchor:** the `FRL.` wordmark and restrained two-level navigation shell

For repository-memory governance see `FRL_DOCUMENTATION_SYNC_CONTRACT.md`.

## Intent

FRL should feel beautiful, expensive, calm and deliberate.

The visual reference is not a generic SaaS dashboard, AI product, sportsbook or game HUD. The closer mental model is a high-end digital product shaped by luxury principles: precision, restraint, confidence, proportion and material quality.

Luxury should come from composition, typography, spacing, hierarchy and detail — not decoration.

> **FRL should look expensive even in black and white.**

## Current material direction

The current intended FRL product language is dark and editorial.

Primary character:

- near-black / charcoal structure;
- warm ivory / cream text;
- muted stone/grey secondary hierarchy;
- restrained coral/orange brand/action emphasis;
- olive/green reserved for meaningful positive/supporting semantics;
- subtle blue/grey support where useful;
- thin dividers and precise spacing.

Do not use lime green as general FRL branding.

The codebase is still migrating from the earlier warm parchment token system. Legacy tokens or older light surfaces are implementation history, not a reason to pull new work back toward the superseded palette.

## Brand mark — `FRL.`

The primary FRL logo is the compact typographic wordmark **`FRL.`**.

- The full stop is part of the mark.
- The mark should remain compact, bold and tightly tracked.
- **Football Research Laboratory** is supporting descriptor copy, not part of the core mark.
- Do not embellish it with icons, crests, gradients or decorative symbols.

## Digital × luxury

**Digital** contributes speed, clarity, precision, data density, interaction and analytical capability.

**Luxury** contributes restraint, composition, typography, proportion, scarcity of signals, confidence and attention to detail.

The aim is not a fashion-house pastiche. It is a serious football research product with the discipline and finish of a luxury digital brand.

The shell, contextual navigation and page body should feel like one composition. Shared content rails matter.

## Hard rejection rules

Reject a design if its main language depends on:

- neon or glowing borders;
- glassmorphism;
- gradient-heavy spectacle;
- floating dashboard cards everywhere;
- oversized KPI tiles;
- excessive pills, badges or chips;
- icon-heavy navigation;
- gamified HUD styling;
- generic rounded SaaS controls;
- heavy shadows;
- decorative animation without analytical purpose;
- rainbow metric colouring;
- large empty hero sections without product purpose;
- component grids that make the page feel assembled rather than composed.

A dark theme is not permission to become a generic 'premium dashboard'.

## Positive design principles

### 1. Compose pages; do not stack components

Use hierarchy, alignment, whitespace and rules before containers.

### 2. Typography carries status

Important football information should feel editorial rather than labelled by UI chrome. Large type is reserved for genuinely important identity, result or conclusion.

### 3. Whitespace is functional

Whitespace separates levels of meaning and improves scanning. It is not wasted space.

### 4. Use rules before boxes

Prefer hairline dividers, alignment and spacing over bordered containers.

### 5. Colour is scarce

Most interfaces should remain convincing in near-monochrome. Coral/orange is a controlled emphasis; green/olive and other support colours need a reason.

### 6. Interaction is quiet

Hover, selection and focus should feel precise. Avoid bounce, glow, gratuitous scaling or motion.

### 7. Football first

Team names, crests, stadiums, kits, scores, dates, match context and analytical conclusions dominate. Interface labels recede.

### 8. Data density can still feel premium

Tables, rankings, fixtures and research evidence may be dense. Typeset them like a serious publication/product rather than an admin console.

### 9. Contextual complexity

Global navigation stays simple. Detailed controls appear only where relevant to the current task.

### 10. Provenance stays available

Evidence quality, missingness and methodology remain inspectable without overwhelming the football task.

## Current reference surfaces

### Fixtures

Fixtures should behave like a premium football archive / sporting ledger:

- strong but restrained club identity;
- elegant season/context control;
- month/round rhythm;
- natural match rows;
- result/score emphasis;
- upcoming fixtures quieter than completed matches;
- provenance and coverage visually secondary but available.

### Fixture result

A result page may be more cinematic than a ledger while preserving analytical clarity. Football identity and result hierarchy come first; supporting evidence follows.

### Matchday / Fixture Intelligence

Matchday is a research workspace, not a sportsbook imitation. Governed evidence may be betting-relevant without collapsing descriptive evidence, probability, market price and strategy into one visual concept.

### Team Profile

Team Profile may use richer crest/stadium identity but remains an entity profile, not a giant statistics dashboard. It should route naturally into analytical surfaces.

These are reference applications of the constitution, not templates to copy mechanically onto every page.

## Product character

FRL should feel:

- assured rather than flashy;
- digitally precise rather than futuristic for its own sake;
- analytical rather than gamified;
- dark and warm rather than sterile;
- crafted rather than generated;
- premium rather than ornate;
- distinctive rather than trend-led.

## Final test

If a page could plausibly be mistaken for a generic AI startup template, sportsbook skin or dashboard theme pack, redesign it.

The reusable standard is:

> **digital precision × editorial football identity × luxury restraint**
